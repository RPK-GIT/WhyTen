# VISUAL WORLDS — structural classification (v1)

Four visual categories. **Classification only — no final visual designs yet**
(designs are Gates 5–6). Every future scene declares exactly one primary world;
transitions between worlds are deliberate storytelling events.

## 1. REAL WORLD
Nia's everyday reality, where the mystery hides in plain sight.
Manuscript anchors: the Saturday bedroom crowded with hidden tens (p.1), the wall
clock (p.2), school and passed notes (p.22), the wristwatch (p.17), phones (p.23),
streets and shops (p.1).

## 2. IMAGINED WORLD
Worlds the characters invent — a notebook page becoming a world.
Manuscript anchors: Pentaria — five-petalled flowers, five-sided houses, one-handed
citizens, market bags of five (pp.4–5); the bedroom-turned-noir detective office (p.7);
the game-show set (p.19).

## 3. HISTORICAL WORLD
Real past civilisations and the anthropological investigation.
Manuscript anchors: Babylonian astronomer on a ziggurat (pp.2, 17), Maya scribe (p.2),
the world map of counting systems glowing by continent (p.10), the gallery of wrong
scholars (p.18). All content here is subject to `qa/historical/HISTORICAL_QA_FRAMEWORK.md`.

## 4. ABSTRACT MATHEMATICAL WORLD
Mathematics made visible, outside any location.
Manuscript anchors: the bedrock-and-dial cutaway machine (p.12), the hands-into-digits
reveal spread (p.13), the necessity/convention bins (p.16), bags-and-loose mechanics
(pp.4–5), the cathedral-dark binary space of switches (p.23).

## Classification rules

1. One primary world per scene; a scene may *transition* worlds (e.g. bedroom → notebook
   → Pentaria) but the transition itself is scripted and boarded.
2. Recurring cross-world objects (hands, bags, the dial, the corkboard) are reusable
   assets (`assets/ASSET_STRATEGY.md`) restyled per world, not redesigned per scene.
3. Each world gets its own style definition at Gate 5; until then, no world-specific
   look is approved.
