# STORY ARCHITECTURE — v1 (Gate 2 candidate)

**Status: DRAFT — awaiting Gate 2 approval.**
High-level architecture only. Not a beat sheet, not a screenplay.
Every act cites its manuscript pages; the manuscript wording governs.

## Spine

The audience is made to *suspect ten is mathematically special*, then investigates
alongside Nia and Sam until the evidence forces the reveal — ten is a human
convention written on our hands — and leaves with a reusable lens:
**necessity vs convention: did the universe insist, or did we?**

## Acts

### ACT 1 — THE MYSTERY (manuscript pp.1–3)
Ten appears everywhere in Nia's ordinary Saturday. The clock betrays sixty;
Babylon, Maya twenties, and binary computers exist as ghosts of other counting worlds.
Sam's ink-signed question: **Is ten actually special? YES or NO.**
Nia's reset-the-world question: if numbers were forgotten tonight, would ten win again?

### ACT 2 — BREAK TEN (pp.4–6)
Nia and Sam invent **Pentaria**: one-handed citizens, bags of five.
Thirteen beans → two bags, three loose → written **23**. Market arithmetic:
4 + 3 berries → a bag snaps shut → **12** (our seven). Carrying happens by itself.
Sam tests everything; nothing breaks. Conclusion said plainly:
**mathematics does not care about ten.** Sam crosses out his YES.

### ACT 3 — THE DETECTIVE CASE (pp.7–9)
Egypt, China, India, Rome — civilisations that never met all landed on ten. That's a clue.
The suspect board: (1) roundest/friendliest number, (2) a king decreed it,
(3) everyone copied one village, (4) *fingers?* — plus a blank card.
The rule that powers the act: **a hypothesis must make a prediction.**
Each suspect sticks its neck out; the fingers card predicts that people who counted
on their bodies differently should have landed on different numbers.

### ACT 4 — THE EVIDENCE (pp.10–11)
The anthropological files: counting systems of hundreds of cultures cluster at
**fives, tens, twenties** — one hand, two hands, hands and feet.
Two apparent misfits investigated and cracked:
**Yuki base eight** — counted in the *gaps between* fingers (4 + 4).
**Babylon base sixty** — twelve finger-segments counted by the thumb, tallied
on five fingers of the other hand: 12 × 5 = 60. Even the exceptions are hands.

### ACT 5 — THE ANSWER (pp.12–13)
First the shape: grouping is **bedrock** (required); bag size is a **dial**
(2 · 5 · 8 · 12 · 20 · 60 … pointer on 10). Then the summit, treated as a major
cinematic reveal: **"We count in tens because we count on our hands."**
The oldest calculator on Earth was at the end of every arm.

### ACT 6 — NECESSITY VS CONVENTION (pp.14, 16)
Why ten *felt* like a law: the worn-shoe effect — anything done ten thousand times
feels like a law of nature. The two bins named: **necessity** (grouping, carrying)
vs **convention** (bag size, digit shapes). The term **base** is introduced.
The lens: *did the universe insist, or did we?*

### ACT 7 — THE BETTER NUMBER (p.15)
Twelve — the number that should probably have won. 12 splits cleanly among 2, 3, 4;
10 mostly doesn't. Dozens in bakeries. Why we don't switch: **historical inertia** —
"try getting eight billion people to change shoes at the same time."
Winners aren't always best; sometimes they're first to get comfortable.

### ACT 8 — THE WORLD STILL USES OLD CHOICES (p.17)
Babylon on your wrist: 60 seconds, 60 minutes, 360 degrees. The empire fell;
its base sixty keeps perfect time. Conventions outlive their inventors and
wear a law's uniform. (p.18's history of famous wrong suspects supports this act's
theme: wrong guesses are the road, not the opposite, of discovery.)

### ACT 9 — ZIB (pp.20–22)
A letter with off-planet postage: **Zib**, eight-fingered alien, boasts of **34** gleeps.
The audience's final application — convert base eight to ours, write back in Zib's
counting. Then the secret-code payoff: birthdays in base five, notes nobody at
school can read. Four thousand years ago turning the dial took an empire.

### ACT 10 — THE MACHINE THAT COUNTS TO TWO (p.23)
The smallest setting on the dial: **two**. On and off — the only alphabet a machine
can feel — and it holds every photo, song, and film on Earth.

### ACT 11 — THE LENS (pp.19, 24)
Return to Nia and Sam. The game-show test of the lens (necessity or convention —
five statements, no mercy) and the three things the mystery leaves behind:
a sentence, a lens, and a detective's badge with a blank third line.
Final beat for the viewer: **"Did the universe insist, or did we?"**
